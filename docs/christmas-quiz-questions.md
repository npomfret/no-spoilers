# Christmas Quiz Questions

Editorial question bank. Subject matter: 2000 onward, inclusive.

Use four-option multiple-choice questions with one correct answer. Aim for fun,
little-known trivia, oddities, outliers and surprising statistics. Each answer should
come with an entertaining explanation and sources.

Record direct source links and a checked date for every question, including the evidence
behind any statistic. Current-season drafts below use the 2026 season through Las Vegas;
they do not assume Qatar or Abu Dhabi has happened.

## Question 1: Expensive extras

**Status:** Approved by the user.
**Year:** 2004.

At Monaco in 2004, Jaguar decided its cars’ noses needed which unusual accessory?

- **A.** A piece of Moon rock
- **B.** A diamond
- **C.** A solid-gold lucky coin
- **D.** A meteorite

**Correct answer: B — A diamond.**

The promotion tied in with *Ocean’s Twelve*. After Christian Klien crashed, Jaguar
reported that his car returned without its diamond. A heist-film promotion became
a missing-jewel mystery.

**Sources checked:** 2026-11-23.

- [ABC News — Diamonds not forever for Jaguar](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2004-05-24/diamonds-not-forever-for-jaguar/1981124),
  published 24 May 2004; contemporary reporting includes Jaguar's statement.
- [Formula 1 — Special Monaco Grand Prix liveries](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/superman-star-wars-and-the-missing-diamond-5-special-monaco-grand-prix.7pcegm7Lmuehn5sfoIHKR7),
  published 17 May 2021; retrospective account of the promotion.

**Fact-check note:** The question asks what was attached to the cars. The explanation
describes Jaguar's report at the time; it does not assert how the jewel disappeared
or what ultimately happened to it.

## Question 2: Six laps of fame

**Status:** Approved by the user.
**Year:** 2007.

In his only F1 race, Markus Winkelhock qualified last and led more laps than the
eventual winner. Which team was he driving for at the 2007 European Grand Prix?

- **A.** Super Aguri
- **B.** Toro Rosso
- **C.** Spyker
- **D.** Williams

**Correct answer: C — Spyker.**

Markus Winkelhock qualified last, gambled on wet tyres before the start, and found
himself leading as the rain hit. He was credited with six laps in front; eventual
winner Fernando Alonso led five. It was Winkelhock's only F1 race, giving him a
100% record for leading races. Sample size: one.

### Research table: laps led at the 2007 European Grand Prix

This table supports authoring and fact-checking; it is not part of the question prompt.
It lists the five drivers credited with leading a lap in this 60-lap race, including
laps behind the safety car. It does not count overtakes or measure racing pace.

| Driver | Team | Laps led | Laps credited as leader |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Felipe Massa | Ferrari | 47 | 8–12, 14–55 |
| Markus Winkelhock | Spyker | 6 | 2–7 |
| Fernando Alonso | McLaren | 5 | 56–60 |
| Kimi Räikkönen | Ferrari | 1 | 1 |
| David Coulthard | Red Bull | 1 | 13 |
| **Total** | | **60** | **1–60** |

**Sources checked:** 2026-11-23.

- [STATS F1 — Europe 2007: Laps led](https://www.statsf1.com/en/2007/europe/en-tete.aspx):
  counts and lap ranges for the table.
- [STATS F1 — Europe 2007: Lap by lap](https://www.statsf1.com/en/2007/europe/tour-par-tour.aspx):
  checked the leader sequence against the summary; inclusive ranges sum to 60 laps.
- [STATS F1 — Europe 2007: Result](https://www.statsf1.com/en/2007/europe/classement.aspx):
  confirms Alonso as the winner.
- [Motor Sport — Markus Winkelhock: The day I led the European GP in a Spyker](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/articles/single-seaters/f1/markus-winkelhock-the-day-i-led-the-european-gp-in-a-spyker/),
  published 22 July 2020; interview with Winkelhock confirms his sole race, last-place
  qualifying and the tyre gamble.
- [Formula 1 — Father-son duos: The Winkelhocks](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/from-world-champions-to-a-unique-f1-record-holder-16-father-son-duos-in-formula-1.2dlsyoNxXRlJj2XCNYevwP),
  published 16 April 2026; corroborates the one-race career and six laps led.

**Fact-check note:** Use “qualified last,” because he changed tyres at the end of
the formation lap and started from the pit lane. Leading the restart is not an
official pole position. Avoid a career-laps percentage: sources use different lap
totals around the interrupted race. The race-level statistic is simply 1/1 = 100%.

## Question 3: Rainbow Road, Nevada

**Status:** Draft for user review.
**Year:** 2026 — Las Vegas Grand Prix.

Vegas was so chaotic that Max Verstappen compared the race to which video game?

- **A.** Gran Turismo
- **B.** Need for Speed
- **C.** Mario Kart
- **D.** F1 26

**Correct answer: C — Mario Kart.**

Asked when he had last won a race like that, Verstappen reached for a video-game
comparison: Mario Kart, where dropping back can help you catch up. Apparently the
Las Vegas Strip had become Rainbow Road. The Rainbow Road line is our joke, not his quote.

**Sources checked:** 2026-11-23.

- [FIA — 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix post-race press conference transcript](https://api.fia.com/news/f1-2026-las-vegas-grand-prix-post-race-press-conference-transcript),
  dated 21 November 2026; the opening question under “PRESS CONFERENCE” and Verstappen's
  answer explicitly name Mario Kart and describe the catch-up effect.

## Question 4: The leader who kept getting overtaken

**Status:** Draft for user review.
**Year:** 2026 — Las Vegas Grand Prix.

The 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix had 35 reported lead changes. Yet Max Verstappen was
first at the end of how many of its 50 laps?

- **A.** 15
- **B.** 25
- **C.** 35
- **D.** 45

**Correct answer: D — 45.**

He was first at 90% of the lap endings, despite the frantic fight for the lead.
End-of-lap statistics do not capture every pass and repass within a lap. A tidy
table can hide a thoroughly untidy race.

| Driver | Laps credited as leader | Laps led |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Max Verstappen | 1–27, 29–30, 32–35, 38–42, 44–50 | 45 |
| George Russell | 28, 31, 36–37 | 4 |
| Kimi Antonelli | 43 | 1 |
| **Total** | **1–50** | **50** |

**Sources checked:** 2026-11-23.

- [STATS F1 — Las Vegas 2026: Laps led](https://www.statsf1.com/en/2026/las-vegas/en-tete.aspx):
  lap-end leadership totals and ranges.
- [STATS F1 — Las Vegas 2026: Lap by lap](https://www.statsf1.com/en/2026/las-vegas/tour-par-tour.aspx):
  leader sequence used to check the summary.
- [Formula 1 — Las Vegas Lowdown](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/las-vegas-lowdown-all-the-key-moments-as-verstappen-wins-remarkable-race-and-ferraris-drivers-collide-again.62D9SeuQXSboAspMm7N0pN),
  published 22 November 2026; reports 35 lead changes across 50 laps.
- [FIA — Las Vegas post-race press conference](https://api.fia.com/news/f1-2026-las-vegas-grand-prix-post-race-press-conference-transcript):
  corroborates the reported 35 changes in the question to Verstappen.

**Fact-check note:** The 35 reported changes and the lap-end leader table are different
measures. Do not describe 35 as the number of lap-end changes or as 35 verified on-track
overtakes. Recomputing the inclusive ranges gives 45 + 4 + 1 = 50; 45/50 = 90%.

## Question 5: Feeling old yet?

**Status:** Approved by the user.
**Year:** 2026, with a connection to 2006.

How many F1 world titles had Fernando Alonso won when Kimi Antonelli was born?

- **A.** None
- **B.** One
- **C.** Two
- **D.** Three

**Correct answer: B — One.**

Antonelli was born on 25 August 2006. Alonso's second title came on 22 October,
almost two months later. He was already a champion when Kimi arrived, just not
yet a double champion.

**Sources checked:** 2026-11-23.

- [Formula 1 — Kimi Antonelli driver profile](https://www.formula1.com/en/drivers/kimi-antonelli):
  gives his birth date as 25 August 2006.
- [Autosport — Massa wins in Brazil, Alonso is champion](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/massa-wins-in-brazil-alonso-is-champion-4406802/4406802/),
  published 22 October 2006; contemporary report confirms Alonso secured his second title that day.

**Fact-check note:** Compare the exact dates, not just the calendar year. “Two” is a
plausible distractor because Alonso won his second title later in Antonelli's birth year.

## Question 6: Four of a kind

**Status:** Rejected by the user as confusing; retained as research, not part of the quiz.
**Period:** 2000–2025 inclusive.

Between 2000 and 2025, which of these countries produced as many different F1
Grand Prix winners as Germany?

- **A.** France
- **B.** Finland
- **C.** Brazil
- **D.** Netherlands

**Correct answer: B — Finland.**

Four Finns won during that period: Mika Häkkinen, Kimi Räikkönen, Heikki Kovalainen
and Valtteri Bottas. Germany's four were Michael Schumacher, Ralf Schumacher,
Sebastian Vettel and Nico Rosberg. The Schumachers, Vettel and Rosberg sound like
an intimidating collection — but Finland matched the headcount.

### Research table: different winning drivers, 2000–2025

Selected nationalities for the question, not a ranking of every nation. Count a driver
once if they won a World Championship Grand Prix during the period, using their racing
nationality. Exclude Sprints and victories before 2000 or after 2025.

| Racing nationality | Winning drivers in the period | Different winners |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Germany | Michael Schumacher, Ralf Schumacher, Sebastian Vettel, Nico Rosberg | 4 |
| Finland | Mika Häkkinen, Kimi Räikkönen, Heikki Kovalainen, Valtteri Bottas | 4 |
| France | Pierre Gasly, Esteban Ocon | 2 |
| Brazil | Rubens Barrichello, Felipe Massa | 2 |
| Netherlands | Max Verstappen | 1 |

**Sources checked:** 2026-11-24.

- [STATS F1 — Finland's wins](https://www.statsf1.com/en/finland/victoire.aspx):
  filter the dated race rows to 2000–2025; the four Finnish names above remain.
- [STATS F1 — Germany's wins](https://www.statsf1.com/en/germany/victoire.aspx):
  the same filter leaves the two Schumachers, Vettel and Rosberg.
- [STATS F1 — France's wins](https://www.statsf1.com/en/france/victoire.aspx):
  Gasly's 2020 win and Ocon's 2021 win are the only qualifying entries.
- [STATS F1 — Brazil's wins](https://www.statsf1.com/en/brazil/victoire.aspx):
  the qualifying entries belong to Barrichello and Massa.
- [STATS F1 — Netherlands](https://www.statsf1.com/en/netherlands.aspx):
  Verstappen is the country's only Grand Prix winner.
- [Formula 1 — Bottas becomes F1's 107th different winner](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/sochi-stats-bottas-f1-107th-different-winner.4Pmgdj9udaueKIGyeS8a6C),
  published 30 April 2017; independently lists the Finnish winners, including Keke Rosberg,
  whose victories are outside our period.

**Fact-check note:** This counts different winners, not victories or champions. Häkkinen
qualifies because he won in 2000 and 2001 even though his first win was earlier. Keke
Rosberg and Heinz-Harald Frentzen are excluded because their wins pre-date 2000. Nico
Rosberg counts for Germany. The table's 13 named drivers are the full inventory for these
five selected nations within the fixed period; only Finland matches Germany among the options.

## Question 7: One man, three champions

**Status:** Draft for user review.
**Cutoff:** End of the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix, 22 November 2026.

Add together the F1 Grand Prix wins of Sebastian Vettel, Fernando Alonso and
Kimi Räikkönen. How does their combined total compare with Lewis Hamilton's?

- **A.** Hamilton leads by one win
- **B.** The trio leads by one win
- **C.** They are exactly level
- **D.** Hamilton leads by ten wins

**Correct answer: C — They are exactly level.**

Vettel's 53 + Alonso's 32 + Räikkönen's 21 = **106**, exactly Hamilton's total.
One driver matches three world champions combined. And the symmetry goes further:
their world titles add up to seven too — 4 + 2 + 1, matching Hamilton's seven.

### Research table: career totals at the cutoff

| Driver | Grand Prix wins | World titles |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Lewis Hamilton | 106 | 7 |
| Sebastian Vettel | 53 | 4 |
| Fernando Alonso | 32 | 2 |
| Kimi Räikkönen | 21 | 1 |
| Vettel + Alonso + Räikkönen | 106 | 7 |

**Sources checked:** 2026-11-24, using the local snapshot without new web requests.

- [F1DB v2026.21.0](https://github.com/f1db/f1db/releases/tag/v2026.21.0),
  retained locally at `research/quiz-data/source/f1db.db`. Counted final
  `race_result.position_number = 1` rows joined to `race`, filtered to
  `year >= 2000` and `date <= '2026-11-22'`, for the four named driver IDs.
  The `driver` table's career win totals agree. Counted
  `season_driver_standing.championship_won = 1` rows for the title totals;
  those agree with `driver.total_championship_wins`.
- Local reusable export: `research/quiz-data/v2026.21.0/driver_stats_2000_plus.csv`,
  with its saved SQL and checksummed manifest. This is derived from the same source,
  not independent corroboration. [Local dataset guide](christmas-quiz-data.md).

**Fact-check note:** Grand Prix victories only; Sprints excluded. All four drivers'
career wins and titles fall within our 2000-onward era, so the period filter does
not truncate any of their totals. The exact tie is fixed to this cutoff and needs
rechecking if the question is updated after another race. Retain as a draft pending
user approval and independent source corroboration before publication.
